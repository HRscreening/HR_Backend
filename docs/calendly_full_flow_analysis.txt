===================================================================================
CALENDLY — CAN WE PUT THE ENTIRE INTERVIEW SCHEDULING FLOW ON CALENDLY?
===================================================================================
Date: March 1, 2026
Author: DeskZero Engineering
Premise: Forget Part 1 (our panel form). Forget our availability DB.
         Can we redesign the ENTIRE flow — panelist availability collection
         AND candidate booking — 100% through Calendly?
===================================================================================


-------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 1: WHAT "ALL ON CALENDLY" WOULD LOOK LIKE
-------------------------------------------------------------------

Step 1: HR creates a Calendly Round Robin event type per interview round.
          e.g. "Technical Screen — Job #42"

Step 2: HR adds all panelists as team members in that event type.
        Each panelist connects their Google/Outlook calendar.
        Calendly reads their calendar free/busy status automatically.

Step 3: HR copies the Calendly Round Robin link and emails it to
        the candidate.

Step 4: Candidate opens Calendly page, picks a time slot, books.

Step 5: Calendly fires invitee.created webhook to our backend.
        We update our DB — set interview SCHEDULED, log panelist.

This sounds simple. Let us now examine every point where it breaks.


-------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2: WHERE IT BREAKS — PROBLEM BY PROBLEM WITH EXAMPLES
-------------------------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM 1: EVERY PANELIST NEEDS A PAID CALENDLY ACCOUNT
--------------------------------------------------------

For Calendly Round Robin to work, every panelist must:
  - Have a Calendly account
  - Be on a paid Calendly Teams or Professional plan
  - Connect their Google/Outlook calendar to Calendly

Pricing (2026 estimate): ~$16/member/month (Teams plan)

Example:
  Your platform has 3 companies as clients.
  Company A has 4 panelists.
  Company B has 6 panelists.
  Company C has 3 panelists.
  Total = 13 panelists × $16 = $208/month

  This is not a DeskZero cost — you would need to either:
    a) Ask each client company to buy Calendly seats for their
       panelists (friction, they may refuse or already use a
       different tool)
    OR
    b) Buy the seats yourself and bill it back (ongoing ops cost)

  As you scale to 50 client companies, this becomes a
  significant and unchained cost your product inherits.

  In our custom system:
    Zero per-panelist cost.
    Panelists just open an email link — no account needed.


PROBLEM 2: PANELISTS MUST SET AVAILABILITY IN CALENDLY, NOT YOUR APP
----------------------------------------------------------------------

In the "all on Calendly" model, panelists no longer use your form.
Instead, they manage their availability inside Calendly's own UI
(or by connecting their Google/Outlook calendar).

This breaks your product experience in several ways:

  A. You lose control of the availability collection UX.
     Your branded panel form is gone. Panelists are redirected
     to Calendly's interface to manage their schedule — a third
     party tool they may not know, want, or have access to.

  B. You lose the `available_slots` data in your DB entirely.
     Right now, panelist_availability table stores exactly what
     slots each panelist confirmed for each specific interview.
     With Calendly, you have no record of this. If a panelist
     says "I was supposed to be available at 2 PM on March 10"
     and a dispute arises, you have no audit trail.

  C. Availability is per-calendar, not per-interview-round.
     In Calendly, a panelist's availability is their GENERAL
     calendar availability — not "I'm available for Application
     #1001's Technical Round between March 10–15."

     Example:
       Alice sets herself as available Mondays 2–4 PM in Calendly.
       This means she's available for ALL candidates for ALL rounds
       on ALL jobs she's assigned to, every Monday 2–4 PM.
       She cannot say "I'm only available for THIS interview on
       THIS specific date range."

       In our system:
         Alice submits availability specifically for interview_id=XYZ
         for round_config_id=ABC. Her slot is scoped to that interview.
         Other interviews don't touch her slot. It expires when that
         interview is scheduled.

  D. Calendly reads Google/Outlook calendar free/busy.
     If Alice has a personal lunch marked as "busy" on her Google
     calendar on March 10 at 1 PM, Calendly will hide that slot.
     BUT if she forgot to block it, Calendly will show it as
     available even if she's actually unavailable that day.
     Your system asks her explicitly: "What slots work for you?"
     Calendly infers: "What does her calendar say?"
     Explicit is more reliable than inferred for interview scheduling.


PROBLEM 3: ONE CALENDLY EVENT TYPE PER INTERVIEW ROUND PER JOB
---------------------------------------------------------------

Calendly has no concept of "rounds." Each event type is a flat,
standalone meeting template.

To represent your data model in Calendly, you would need to create:
  One Calendly Round Robin event type per interview round per job.

Example:
  Job: Senior Backend Engineer (Job #42)
    Round 1: Technical Screen → Calendly event: "Job42-Round1"
    Round 2: System Design    → Calendly event: "Job42-Round2"
    Round 3: Culture Fit      → Calendly event: "Job42-Round3"

  Job: Product Manager (Job #43)
    Round 1: Case Study       → Calendly event: "Job43-Round1"
    Round 2: HR Discussion    → Calendly event: "Job43-Round2"

  5 jobs × 3 rounds average = 15 Calendly event types to create,
  name, configure panelists for, and manage.

  When a job is closed, those 3 event types must be deactivated.
  When a panelist changes, the event type must be updated.
  When a round's duration changes (e.g. from 60 min to 45 min),
  the Calendly event type must be manually updated.

  All of this is manual or requires API calls to manage dynamically.

  In our system:
    interview_round_configs table.
    One row per round per job.
    Duration, instructions, panelists — all in one DB record.
    Updating it is a single PATCH /api/interview/update-round-config.


PROBLEM 4: NO ROUND PROGRESSION GATING
---------------------------------------

In our system:
  move_to_round() in application_service.py enforces:
    - Candidate cannot be in Round 2 until Round 1 is COMPLETED
    - Round must not be expired (start_date/end_date check)
    - Round config must have panelists assigned
    - Application must be in the right status

  Only after these checks pass does the candidate receive any
  booking link at all.

In the Calendly model:
  You have three Calendly links for three rounds.
  Calendly has no concept of "unlock Round 2 only after Round 1 done."

  You must still maintain all of this gating logic in your own
  backend and only send the Round 2 Calendly link after Round 1
  is confirmed complete.

  So you still write all of move_to_round(). You just send a
  Calendly URL instead of your own booking page URL.
  The complexity savings are zero.

  And if you are sending the link from your own backend anyway,
  you can just as easily send your own booking page link.


PROBLEM 5: LINK SECURITY — THE GENERIC LINK PROBLEM
-----------------------------------------------------

When HR uses Calendly, the link they send to a candidate is:
  https://calendly.com/deskzero-portal/job42-round1

This is the SAME link for every candidate applying to Job #42 Round 1.

Example:
  Candidate Rahul — Application #1001 — receives this link.
  Candidate Priya — Application #1002 — receives the same link.
  Rahul forwards his link to his friend Amit.
  Amit books a slot.
  Calendly confirms Amit's booking.
  Our webhook receives: name="Amit Kumar", email="amit@gmail.com"

  Amit has no application in our system.
  We cannot map this booking to any application_id.
  The slot is consumed. A panelist is blocked off.
  Neither Rahul nor Priya got their interview.
  The panelist wasted a slot on a non-candidate.

  Workaround attempt — utm_source param:
    Send Rahul:  .../job42-round1?utm_source=app_1001
    Send Priya:  .../job42-round1?utm_source=app_1002

    But utm_source is a plain text query param, not a signed token.
    Rahul can change utm_source=app_1001 to utm_source=app_1002.
    Calendly passes it through to the webhook as-is.
    There is no verification. Anyone can forge it.

  In our system:
    booking_token = JWT signed with SECRET_KEY
    Payload: { interview_id, application_id, type, exp }
    Tampering the token → signature verification fails → 401.
    Forwarding the token → same application_id is used → we detect
    interview already SCHEDULED → 409.
    Expiry → 401.
    Full security with no external dependency.


PROBLEM 6: WHAT YOUR WEBHOOK HANDLER MUST DO
---------------------------------------------

Even in the "all on Calendly" model, you still need a backend.
When Calendly fires invitee.created, your webhook handler must:

  1. Parse the event payload
  2. Extract panelist email (assigned_to field in payload)
  3. Extract candidate name + email
  4. Extract utm_source if you're using it as application_id hint
  5. Query your DB to find the right application + interview
  6. Verify the application is in the right status
  7. Update interview: status=SCHEDULED, panelist_email, scheduled_start
  8. Log interview_timeline_events record
  9. Send confirmation email to candidate (from your email service)
  10. Send confirmation email to panelist (from your email service)
  11. Handle retries — Calendly fires the webhook again if you return
      a non-200 response; your handler must be idempotent

  That is a full backend service to maintain.

  Compare to our custom endpoint POST /api/candidate/book-slot:
  Steps 1–11 above happen inside one function, one DB transaction,
  no external retry logic, no webhook delivery uncertainty.

  The webhook approach requires you to build:
    - Webhook receiver endpoint
    - Idempotency key handling (to prevent double-processing)
    - Retry queue (if your handler fails, Calendly may not retry forever)
    - Backfill job (if webhook was missed, how do you reconcile?)

  These are solved problems — but they are non-trivial engineering
  on top of what is already a solved problem in our custom system.


PROBLEM 7: CALENDLY INVITEE.CANCELLED — WHAT HAPPENS ON CANCELLATION?
----------------------------------------------------------------------

When a candidate cancels on Calendly, Calendly fires invitee.cancelled.

Your webhook must:
  - Find the interview in your DB
  - Revert interview.status to READY_TO_BOOK
  - Clear scheduled_start, scheduled_end, panelist_email
  - Free up the panelist's slot (but panelist_availability JSONB
    in your DB doesn't track which slot was "used" by Calendly —
    that data lives only in Calendly)
  - Log BOOKING_CANCELLED event

The "which slot was freed" problem:
  In our system, interview_slots table has a row per slot.
  On cancellation: UPDATE interview_slots SET is_booked=False WHERE id=slot_id
  That slot immediately becomes available for other candidates.

  In Calendly:
    The slot is freed inside Calendly automatically.
    But your DB has no interview_slots table (since Calendly owns them).
    You have no record of the freed slot to re-offer to another candidate.
    If Candidate #1 cancels, does a new booking link go to Candidate #2?
    Who sends it? How? Calendly doesn't know about Candidate #2.

  Rescheduling requires re-triggering your entire flow from the
  backend (generate new Calendly link or your own link, send email,
  etc.) — which is identical to what our custom system does.

SECTION 3: WHAT CALENDLY GENUINELY REPLACES IN THIS SCENARIO
-------------------------------------------------------------------

If we put everything on Calendly and accept all trade-offs above,
here is what Calendly actually replaces:

  REPLACED:
    The candidate-facing booking page (a date picker UI)
    The slot availability API (GET /api/candidate/booking-form)
    The slot selection endpoint (POST /api/candidate/book-slot)
    The interview_slots table

  NOT REPLACED (you still build all of this):
    panelist_availability collection (move to Calendly UI = new UX problem)
    move_to_round() gating logic
    JWT booking token security OR accept insecure utm params
    Webhook receiver + idempotency + retry handling
    interview_timeline_events logging
    Confirmation email sending (Calendly sends its own basic email but
    not your branded, data-rich email with candidate name, round info, etc.)
    Cancellation and rescheduling re-triggering
    Calendly event type management per job per round
    Per-seat billing management

  The things Calendly replaces = ~3–4 days of backend + frontend work.
  The things it does NOT replace = the full system minus 3–4 days.

  You are paying $16/panelist/month permanently to save 3–4 days once.

SECTION 5: FINAL ANSWER
-------------------------------------------------------------------

Yes, you CAN technically put the full flow on Calendly.
It would work at small scale with one or two panelists per round
and candidates who are trusted not to share or tamper links.

It would NOT work reliably when:
  - Multiple panelists have overlapping slots (capacity halved)
  - Candidates forward their links (no application-scoped security)
  - Webhooks are delayed or dropped (orphaned interview records)
  - You need a full audit trail (panelist_availability data gone)
  - You have many jobs/rounds (N event types to manage in Calendly)
  - Your client companies refuse to buy Calendly seats for panelists

What you are trading:
  Save:  3–4 days of building a booking page + date picker frontend
  Cost:  $16/panelist/month + webhook complexity + security gaps +
         loss of data ownership + loss of UX control

Part 1 is already built. The booking page is the only remaining piece.
That is the smallest part of this system — not the right moment to
hand control to an external vendor.

CONCLUSION: Do not put the full flow on Calendly.
Build the booking page. Own the full pipeline.
